The Lord of Kamalā, that is, the husband of Śrī, "jayati" — stands supreme. For the verb jayati, when it bears the sense of eminence, is intransitive, as it has been said: "Of the root ji, in its two senses of victory and overpowering, in the first sense it is intransitive." Now, in order to establish the authoritativeness of the revered Tantrasāra, he declares the excellence of its speaker and of its hearer: the verse "vidhiṃ vidhāya." At the beginning of creation — at the commencement of the world's emanation — having "made" Vidhi, that is Brahmā, having brought him forth from the lotus of His own navel, the Deva — Śrī Viṣṇu, possessed of manifold attributes such as divine play — "spoke": He instructed Brahmā. Of what sort was He? "Questioned by him," by that Brahmā; supply "the Tantrasāra" (as the content asked about), since the verb "to ask" governs two objects. Again, of what sort? "His bud-like feet gleaming upon the lap of Ramā" — He whose two sprout-soft feet lie shining on Ramā's lap. Thus, seated wholly at ease, He taught Vidhi, His own son; hence there is no room whatever for the suspicion of deception or the like (in this teaching). (2)

What did He say? The verse "aham." He whose qualities are akhila — full, infinite — is so described. Such a one am I, eka, alone; verily no other is such. As the Amarakośa says: "eka means chief, other, and sole." The praṇava is My vācaka, My designator — that which declares My possession of infinite qualities. That same praṇava "begins with akāra and ends with atiśānta": akāra stands at its beginning and atiśānta at its end. Through its division into akāra, ukāra, makāra, bindu, nāda, ghoṣa, śānta and atiśānta, it is "held" — known, celebrated — as having eight syllables. (3)

My other fifty mūrtis, beginning with Aja, are "differentiations of those forms" — differentiations of the forms of those deities of the eight syllables, Viśva and the rest. By elimination, the sense is that they are manifested out of them. They are "expressible by the fifty letters" in order; and the fifty letters are "differentiated by the syllables of the tāra" — that is, manifested out of the syllables of the praṇava. (5)

गोविन्दको गपशचैः पशचैश्च गेन विष्णुः तथा शपगचैश्च मधोर्निहन्ता ॥ १ ॥
त्रैविक्रमो गचशपैः चगपद्मशङ्खैः स्याद्वामनः चगशपैः कमलाधरोऽपि ।
आख्यायते चपशगैश्च हृषीकपो च स्यात्पद्मनाभ इह पेन चगेन शेन ॥ २ ॥
दामोदरः शगचपैः शपचक्रगैश्च सङ्कर्षणः शचपगैरथ वासुदेवः ।
प्रद्युम्नकः शगपचैरनिरुद्धरूपः ख्यातो गदाशपचकैः पुरुषोत्तमोऽधात् ॥ ३ ॥
बाहुस्थितैः पशगचैः गशचक्रपद्मैः ख्यातो ह्यधोक्षज इहाथ नृपञ्चवक्त्रः ।
पद्मं गदां च जलजं सुसुदर्शनं च विभ्रद्भुजैः पचशगैः कथितोऽच्युतोऽपि ॥ ४ ॥
उक्तो जनार्दन इतीह चशंगपेन कौमोदकी चापदरैरुदितो ह्युपेन्द्रः ।
उक्तो हरिश्चपगशैः गपचैश्च शेन कृष्णः क्रमोऽयमिह दक्षतनूर्ध्वबाहुः ॥ ५ ॥ ॥ २८ ॥
#C-END
#CT
Since, for the six sets of six beginning with Keśava, the conch's place has been stated as fixed, the six arrangements of the remaining three — discus and the rest — through the difference of krama and the other orders, are to be easily understood, in sequence, for the six (sets) beginning with Keśava. "Discus, mace, lotus" is the krama (direct order); "lotus, mace, discus" is the vyutkrama (reverse order); "lotus, discus, mace" is the padma-krama; "mace, discus, lotus" is the gadā-vyutkrama; "discus, lotus, mace" is the ardha-krama (half-order); "mace, lotus, discus" is the sāntara-krama (alternating order). Here are the summary verses (śa = conch, ca = discus, ga = mace, pa = lotus, dara/jalaja = conch, Kaumodakī = mace, Sudarśana = discus): (1) "Keśava is with śa-ca-ga-pa; Nārāyaṇa with pa-ga-ca and śa; then Mādhava too with ca-śā-pa-ga; Govinda with ga-pa-śa-ca; Viṣṇu with pa-śa-ca and ga; and the Slayer of Madhu with śa-pa-ga-ca. (2) Trivikrama with ga-ca-śa-pa; Vāmana is with ca-ga, lotus and conch; the Lotus-bearer (Śrīdhara) too is proclaimed with ca-ga-śa-pa(?); and Hṛṣīkeśa with ca-pa-śa-ga; Padmanābha here with pa, with ca-ga, and with śa. (3) Dāmodara with śa-ga-ca-pa; Saṅkarṣaṇa with śa-pa, discus and ga; then Vāsudeva with śa-ca-pa-ga; Pradyumna with śa-ga-pa-ca; the form of Aniruddha is known by mace, śa-pa and discus; Puruṣottama bore (his emblems) next. (4) By the arms holding pa-śa-ga-ca Adhokṣaja is known; then here the Man-lion-faced (Nṛsiṁha) with mace, conch, discus and lotus; and Acyuta is declared bearing with his arms the lotus, the mace, the conch (jalaja) and the fair Sudarśana — pa-ca-śa-ga. (5) Janārdana is stated here with ca-śa-ga-pa; Upendra is proclaimed with Kaumodakī (mace), discus, lotus and conch; Hari is stated with ca-pa-ga-śa; Kṛṣṇa with ga-pa-ca and śa. This order here begins from the upper right arm." (28)

#CT

On the joints of the arms and legs together with their tips — five (points on each limb) — go twenty letters; four one should place on the navel, heart, mouth and head — such is the meaning. Here too accompaniment by Keśava and the other letter-deities is to be understood. (42)

#V-END

मातृकापुरुषसूक्तयोः ऋष्यादीनाह । तारवदिति । पञ्चाशदक्षराणां पुरुषसूक्तस्यापि अनुष्टुप् छन्दः । ऋषिदेवते तारवत् प्रणवमन्त्रस्यैव ॥ ४३ ॥
#C-END
#CT

#V

विष्णुशब्दश्चतुर्थ्यन्तो हृदयेतः षडक्षरः ॥ ४४ ॥
#V-END
#T
"Its metres are Anuṣṭubh and Triṣṭubh — or rather the Triṣṭubhs (in the plural); the word 'Viṣṇu' with the dative ending, joined with the hṛdaya (namaḥ), is the six-syllable mantra. (44)
#T-END

#C

#CT

With the word namaḥ as its "heart," and with ṣa and ṇa counted as separate, it has six syllables; even so, "viṣṇave namaḥ" is the form of this mantra, sasrāpatākhavat (this final word is obscure as printed in this edition). (44)

#V-END
#T

#C-END
#CT

एतज्ज्ञानात्समस्तं च ज्ञातं स्याच्छब्दगोचरम् ।

#V-END
#T

#C

#C-END
#CT
He states the stations of the letter-nyāsa in the collective order: "pajjānu." Here, because of the words "as with the tāra," it is to be borne in mind that Viśva and the rest are the deities of the letters; and accordingly the nyāsa is of the letters as combined with them. The very order in which the limb-nyāsas of the praṇava and the rest have been declared is the order to be observed in japa. Some, however, say that the order of japa follows the order of manifestation itself. Many followers of tradition say that only renunciates are entitled to the japa of the praṇava, and not householders. But others say that householders too are entitled, both because mantras recited together with the praṇava are declared to yield greater fruit, and because no prohibition is (anywhere) heard. — "But since the mantras of the Lord are many, how is it that the limbs and the rest are prescribed for these eight alone?" To this he replies "ete tu": these are the mūla-mantras, the primal mantras; for that reason they are taught first. Others as well will be stated in the fourth chapter. (46–47)

#CT

But others say: with the other mantras — those of the pīṭha and the āvaraṇas — the flower-offering is made once, while the homa is fourfold of that flower-offering. (66)

जपध्यानहुतार्चादीनेवं यः कुरुते सदा ।
धर्मार्थकाममोक्षाणां भाजनं स्यात्स एव हि ॥ ६९ ॥
सर्वोत्तमं हरिं ज्ञात्वा य एवं भक्तिपूर्वकम् ।
जपध्यानादिभिर्नित्यं पूजयेन्नास्य दुर्लभम् ॥ ७० ॥
भक्तिं कृत्वाऽन्यदेवेषु ब्रह्मरुद्रादिकेष्वपि ।
सर्वोत्कर्षमविज्ञाय विष्णोर्याति तमो ध्रुवम् ॥ ७१ ॥

दैवतानि च सर्वाणि त्रातुं तं शक्नुयुः क्वचित् ॥ ७२ ॥
हरिर्हि सर्वदेवानां परमः पूर्णशक्तिमान् ।
स्वतन्त्रोऽन्ये तद्वशा हि सर्वेऽतः स जगद्गुरुः ॥ ७३ ॥
#V-END
#T
"He who ever performs japa, meditation, oblation, worship and the rest in this manner — he indeed becomes the vessel of dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa. (69) He who, knowing Hari as the highest of all, thus worships Him daily with devotion, with japa, meditation and the rest — for him nothing is hard to attain. (70) But he who places devotion in other gods — even in Brahmā, Rudra and the rest — without recognizing Viṣṇu's supremacy over all, goes surely to darkness. (71) Neither sacrifices, nor holy fords, nor fasts and vows, nor all the deities together, could ever save him. (72) For Hari is supreme above all gods, possessed of complete power, independent; all others indeed are subject to Him — hence He is the guru of the universe. (73)
