#H ॥ अथ चतुर्थोऽध्यायः ॥
#T
Now the Fourth Chapter.
#T-END

#V
अथ विष्णूदिते तन्त्रसारे मन्त्रगणो हि यः ।
उदितः सङ्ग्रहेणासावुच्यतेऽखिलसाधकः ॥ १ ॥
#V-END
#T
"Now the host of mantras that was declared in the Tantrasāra spoken by Viṣṇu — that host, accomplishing all (ends), is here stated in summary. (1)
#T-END

#C
मन्त्रोद्धारादि वक्तुमुपक्रमते । अथेति । सङ्ग्रहेण सङ्क्षेपेण ॥ १ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He begins to state the extraction of the mantras and the rest: "atha." "Saṅgraheṇa" — in brief. (1)
#CT-END

#V
याष्टमस्तूयुतोऽन्तेन समेतश्चैव भूभृतः ।
बीजं नमश्च भगवान् सोद्देश्यो रूपसंयुतः ॥ २ ॥
स एव तादृशो लोकत्रयपालो भुवः पतेः ।
भावं मे देहि दान्ते तु दापय स्वाहया युतः ॥ ३ ॥
#V-END
#T
"The eighth (of the ya-series, i.e. ha), joined with u, and united with the ending (bindu), is the bīja of the Boar (Varāha); then 'namaḥ,' and 'bhagavān' with the object (in the dative) and joined with (the phrase about) His form; (2) that same one, of such kind, 'protector of the three worlds,' 'of the Lord of the earth,' 'give me the state of' — and at the end 'dāpaya,' joined with 'svāhā.' (3)
#T-END

#C
वराहमन्त्रमुद्धरति । याष्टम इति । यकारादष्टमवर्णो हकारः । उयुतः उकारेण युक्तः । अन्ते बिन्दुना संयुक्तः । भूभृतो वराहस्य बीजम् । तदनु नमः शब्दः भगवान् भगवच्छब्दः उद्देश्यः चतुर्थ्यन्तः रूपसंयुक्तः । स एव वराहशब्द एव तादृशः चतुर्थ्यन्तः । वराहरूपायेति । लोकत्रयपालोऽपि चतुर्थ्यन्तः । तथा च ॐ हुं नमो भगवते वराहरूपाय भूर्भुवःस्वःपतये भूपतित्वं मे देहि ददापय स्वाहेति मन्त्रः । त्रयस्त्रिंशदक्षरोऽयम् ॥ २-३ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He extracts the Varāha-mantra: "yāṣṭamaḥ." The eighth letter from ya is ha. "Uyuta" — joined with u. At the end united with the bindu (ṁ). (This, "huṁ," is) the bīja of the Bhūbhṛt, the Boar. After it, the word "namaḥ"; "bhagavān" — the word bhagavat, as the object, with a dative ending, joined with (the mention of) His form; that same "varāha" word likewise with a dative ending — "varāharūpāya"; "protector of the three worlds" also with a dative ending. And thus the mantra is: "Oṁ huṁ namo bhagavate varāharūpāya bhūrbhuvaḥsvaḥpataye bhūpatitvaṁ me dehi dadāpaya svāhā." This has thirty-three syllables. (2–3)
#CT-END

#V
मनुस्तस्यापरो नामबीजोद्देशनमोयुतः ।
नमः सोद्देशभगवान् महाशब्दयुतश्च सः ।
तादृशः स्वाहया युक्तो वराहस्योपरो मनुः ॥ ४ ॥
#V-END
#T
"Another mantra of his (Varāha) is joined with the name-bīja, the object (in the dative), and 'namaḥ'; 'namaḥ,' 'bhagavān' with the object, and joined with the word 'mahā' — that one too, of such kind, joined with 'svāhā,' is another mantra of the Boar. (4)
#T-END

#C
सप्ताक्षरं वराहमन्त्रान्तरमाह । मनुस्तस्यापर इति । नामबीजं पूर्वोक्तं । उद्देशः चतुर्थी । नमः शब्देन युक्तः । आदौ बीज तत् चतुर्थ्यन्तो वराहशब्दः । तथा च हुं वराहाय नमः इति सप्ताक्षरो मन्त्रः । मन्त्रान्तरमाह । आदौ नमः शब्दः । ततः चतुर्थ्यन्तो भगवच्छब्दः । महाशब्दयुतः सः वराहः । तादृशः सोद्देशः । अन्ते स्वाहापदेन युक्तः । ततश्च नमो भगवते महावराहाय स्वाहेति चतुर्दशाक्षरः तृतीयो वराहमन्त्रः ॥ ४ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states another Varāha-mantra of seven syllables: "manus tasyāparaḥ." The name-bīja is the aforesaid (huṁ). The "uddeśa" is the dative. Joined with the word "namaḥ." At the beginning the bīja, then the word "varāha" with a dative ending. And thus the seven-syllable mantra is "huṁ varāhāya namaḥ." He states another mantra: at the beginning the word "namaḥ"; then the word "bhagavat" with a dative ending; that Varāha joined with the word "mahā"; of such kind, with the object; at the end joined with the word "svāhā." And thus "namo bhagavate mahāvarāhāya svāhā" is the third Varāha-mantra, of fourteen syllables. (4)
#CT-END

#V
श्यामः सुदर्शनदराभयसद्धरेतो
भूम्या युतोऽखिलनिजोक्तपरिग्रहैश्च ।
ध्येयो निजैश्च तनुभिः सकलैरुपेतः
कोलो हरिः सकलवाञ्छितसिद्धयेऽजः ॥ ५ ॥
#V-END
#T
"Dark-hued, holding the Sudarśana (discus), the conch, the gesture of fearlessness, and the boon-giving (hand), together with Bhūmī (Earth) and with all His aforesaid attendants, is to be meditated upon — attended by all His own embodied forms — the Boar, Hari, the Unborn, for the accomplishment of all that is desired. (5)
#T-END

#C
उक्तमन्त्रत्रयस्यापि ध्यानमाह । श्याम इति । सुदर्शनदराभयसद्धरेतः चक्रशङ्खाभयवरमुद्रायुक्तबाहुचतुष्टयः ॥ ५ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the meditation of all three aforesaid mantras: "śyāmaḥ." "Holding the Sudarśana, the conch, fearlessness and the boon" — four-armed, with arms bearing the discus, the conch, the abhaya-mudrā and the vara-mudrā. (5)
#CT-END

#V
वैष्णवानां मुनिर्ब्रह्मा मन्त्राणां वर्णभेदतः ।
ज्ञेयं छन्दो देवतैकस्तत्तद्रूपो हरिः परः ॥ ६ ॥
#V-END
#T
"Of the Vaiṣṇava mantras the sage (ṛṣi) is Brahmā; the metre is to be known according to the number of their letters; and the sole deity is the supreme Hari in His respective form. (6)
#T-END

#C
सर्वमन्त्रसाधारणऋष्यादीनाह । वैष्णवानामिति ॥ ६ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the ṛṣi and the rest common to all the mantras: "vaiṣṇavānām." (6)
#CT-END

#V
प्रणवेन स्वनाम्ना च भूधरेण परात्मना ।
सर्वज्ञसर्वशक्तिभ्यां षडङ्गानि विदो विदुः ॥ ७ ॥
#V-END
#T
"With the praṇava, with His own name, with 'bhūdhara' (the Earth-bearer), with 'parātman,' and with 'sarvajña' and 'sarvaśakti' — the knowers know the six limbs. (7)
#T-END

#C
षडङ्गान्याह । प्रणवेनेति ॥ ७ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the six limbs: "praṇavena." (7)
#CT-END

#V
ज्ञानैश्वर्यप्रभानन्दतेजः शक्तिभिरेव च (वा) ।
पूर्णात्ममध्यगैः सर्ववैष्णवाङ्गान्यथो विदुः ॥ ८ ॥
#V-END
#T
"And with (the terms) knowledge, lordship, radiance, bliss, splendor and power — placed between (the words) 'pūrṇa' and 'ātman' — they know the limbs of all the Vaiṣṇava (mantras). (8)
#T-END

#C
सर्वमन्त्रसाधारणाङ्गान्याह । ज्ञानैश्वर्येति । पूर्णशब्दात्मशब्दमध्यगैः । पूर्णज्ञानात्मने नम इत्यादि । अनुक्ताङ्गेषु मन्त्रेषु एतान्येवाङ्गानीत्यवधेयम् ॥ ८ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the limbs common to all the mantras: "jñānaiśvarya." Placed between the word "pūrṇa" and the word "ātman": "pūrṇajñānātmane namaḥ," and so on. In the mantras whose limbs are not (separately) stated, these very are to be understood as the limbs. (8)
#CT-END

#V
तिष्ठोदादिस्वजायायाः कारणं स्वाहया युतः ।
श्रीकरोऽष्टाक्षरो मन्त्रो गरुडारूढसंस्मृतिः ॥ ९ ॥
#V-END
#T
"'tiṣṭha,' preceded by 'ud,' the 'kāraṇa' (kara) of His own consort Śrī, joined with 'svāhā' — the eight-syllable mantra 'Śrīkara,' (whose deity is) remembered as mounted on Garuḍa. (9)
#T-END

#C
श्रीकरमन्त्रमाह ॥ तिष्ठेति पदं । उदादितिष्ठेति । उत्तिष्ठ । स्वजायायाः श्रीशब्दः । कारणं करशब्दः । स्वाहाशब्देन युतः । तथाच उत्तिष्ठ श्रीकर स्वाहेति अष्टाक्षरोऽयं मन्त्रः गरुडमारूढो ध्येयः ॥ ९ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the Śrīkara-mantra: the word "tiṣṭha," (preceded) by "ud" — "uttiṣṭha." Of His own consort, the word "śrī"; the "kāraṇa," the word "kara"; joined with the word "svāhā." And thus "uttiṣṭha śrīkara svāhā" is this eight-syllable mantra; (its deity) is to be meditated as mounted on Garuḍa. (9)
#CT-END

#V
आदित्यचन्द्रावपि चक्रशङ्खौ शङ्खप्रसूनाख्यनिधी दधानः ।
चिन्त्यः कराभ्यां वसु तर्पयन्नः स्वाङ्कस्थलक्ष्मीर्गरुडांससंस्थः ॥
द्विर्भीषय त्रासय च प्रमर्दय तथैव च ।
प्रध्वंसयाथो रक्षेति पञ्चाङ्गश्चेत्युदाहृतः ॥ १० ॥
#V-END
#T
"Bearing the Sun and the Moon, and the discus and the conch, and the treasures named Śaṅkha and (Padma)-prasūna, to be contemplated as satisfying us with wealth by His (other) two hands, with Lakṣmī seated on His lap, stationed on Garuḍa's shoulder; 'bhīṣaya' twice, and 'trāsaya,' and likewise 'pramardaya,' then 'pradhvaṁsaya,' and 'rakṣa' — thus is (this one) with five limbs declared. (10)
#T-END

#C
अङ्गान्याह । द्विर्भीषयेति । भीषय भीषय हृदयाय नम इत्यादि ॥ १० ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the limbs: "dvir bhīṣaya." "bhīṣaya bhīṣaya — to the heart, namaḥ," and so on. (10)
#CT-END

#V
क्रूरं वीरं बृहद्विष्णुं दीप्यन्तं विश्वतोमुखम् ।
पुंमृगेन्द्रं भयकरं शुभं मृत्योश्च मारकम् ।
नमामि स्वयमित्येष द्वात्रिंशार्णो मनुर्हरेः ॥ ११ ॥
#V-END
#T
"'The fierce, the heroic, the mighty Viṣṇu, blazing, facing every way, the man-lion, terror-causing, auspicious, the slayer even of Death — I bow to (Him) myself': thus this mantra of Hari, of thirty-two syllables. (11)
#T-END

#C
नृसिंहमन्त्रमाह । क्रूरमिति । क्रूरं उग्रं वीरं बृहद्विष्णुं महाविष्णुं दीप्यन्तं ज्वलन्तं विश्वतो मुखं सर्वतो मुखम् । पुंमृगेन्द्रं नृसिंहं । भयङ्करं भीषणं ।
#C-END
#CT

#V
द्विर्जयस्योपरि निजं सोद्देशं सर्वविच्च था ।
महातेजोबलं वीर्यं तादृशं स्वाहया युतम् ।
चतुर्विंशाक्षरो मन्त्रो निखिलेष्टप्रदायकः ॥ १२ ॥
#V-END
#T
"'jaya' twice, after it His own (name) with the object (in the dative), and 'sarvavid,' likewise 'mahātejobala,' 'vīrya' — such (a mantra), joined with 'svāhā,' is a mantra of twenty-four syllables, the bestower of all that is desired. (12)
#T-END

#C
तस्यैव मन्त्रान्तरमाह । द्विर्जयस्येति । जयद्वयस्यापि । सोद्देश्यं चतुर्थ्यन्तं । अन्ते स्वाहापदेन युक्तम् । एवं सति जयजय नरसिंहाय सर्वज्ञाय महातेजोबलवीर्याय स्वाहेति चतुर्विंशत्यक्षरोऽयं मन्त्रः ॥ १२ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states another mantra of that same (Nṛsiṁha): "dvir jayasya." Of the two "jaya"s too; with the object, ending in the dative; at the end joined with the word "svāhā." This being so, "jaya jaya narasiṁhāya sarvajñāya mahātejobalavīryāya svāhā" is this mantra of twenty-four syllables. (12)
#CT-END

#V
विद्महे धीमहे पूर्वं स्वयं चैव महाबलः ।
प्रेरयेत्तन्न इत्येतन्मध्येऽनन्तः प्रकीर्तितः ॥ १३ ॥
#V-END
#T
"'vidmahe,' 'dhīmahi'; before (each) His own (name), and 'mahābala'; 'prerayet,' 'tan naḥ' — in the middle of this, Ananta (the endless one) is proclaimed. (13)
#T-END

#C
नृसिंहगायत्रीमाह । विद्मह इति । विद्महे इत्यतः पूर्वं स्वाङ्गेन । नृसिंहश्चतुर्थ्यन्तः धीमहीत्यतः पूर्वं महाबलपदं चतुर्थ्यन्तं प्रेरयेत् नः । तत्सपदयोर्मध्ये अनन्तशब्दः । तथा च नरसिंहाय विद्महे महाबलाय धीमहि । तन्नोऽनन्तः प्रचोदयात् इति चतुर्विंशत्यक्षरनृसिंहगायत्रीमन्त्रः ॥ १३ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the Nṛsiṁha-gāyatrī: "vidmahe." Before "vidmahe," with His own (name): "nṛsiṁha" with a dative ending; before "dhīmahi," the word "mahābala" with a dative ending; "prerayet naḥ." Between those two words (tat and naḥ), the word "ananta." And thus "narasiṁhāya vidmahe mahābalāya dhīmahi, tan no'nantaḥ pracodayāt" is the Nṛsiṁha-gāyatrī mantra of twenty-four syllables. (13)
#CT-END

#V
पादैर्व्यस्तैः समस्तैश्च पादैश्चाङ्गं प्रकीर्तितम् ।
पूर्वयोस्तत्र पूर्वं तु सर्वज्ञेन षडङ्गकम् ।
तृतीयं तु द्विरावृत्तैः पादैरङ्गसमन्वितम् ॥ १४ ॥
#V-END
#T
"With the pādas (quarters) taken severally and jointly, and with the pādas, the limbs are proclaimed; of the first two (mantras), for the first, the six limbs are with 'sarvajña'; but the third is furnished with limbs by the pādas repeated twice. (14)
#T-END

#C
अङ्गन्यासमाह । पादैरिति । त्रिषु मन्त्रेषु पूर्वयोः मन्त्रयोः पादैः व्यस्तेन समस्तेन च पञ्चाङ्गानि । सर्वज्ञपदेन चतुर्थ्यन्तेन षष्ठं अङ्गं इति । द्वात्रिंशद्वर्णमन्त्रे तृतीयं गायत्रीरूपं द्विरावृत्तैः त्रिभिः पादैरेव षडङ्गमिति ॥ १४ ॥
#C-END
#CT
He states the limb-nyāsa: "pādaiḥ." Of the three mantras, for the first two, the five limbs are with the pādas taken severally and jointly; the sixth limb is with the word "sarvajña" in the dative. In the thirty-two-syllable mantra, the third — in the form of the Gāyatrī — has its six limbs by the three pādas repeated twice. (14)
#CT-END

(137) Recaka, pūraka and kumbhaka — (prāṇāyāma) is recorded as threefold; prāṇāyāma without the remembrance of Viṣṇu is (merely) called niyama. "Iḍā and Piṅgalā, and Vajrikā and Dhāriṇī likewise, in the four directions of the suṣumnā; and there, in the Vajrikā and the rest, that Lord, four-formed, is established in the heart. (154)

#V-END
#T

#C
द्वासप्ततिनाड्यो प्रधानाः । अन्याः वज्रिकाद्यास्त्वप्रधाना इत्यर्थः । आसु ध्येयं वक्तुमाह । बृहतीति । बृहती नवाक्षरपदं बृहतीछन्दः तु ऋचां ये स्पर्शाः वर्णाः कादिमावसानाः पञ्चवर्गस्थाः तेषां ऋचां च वाच्यः । केशवो नानारूपो ध्येय इति ॥ १५५-१५६ ॥

The seventy-two (thousand) channels are principal; the others, Vajrikā and the rest, are subsidiary — such is the sense. He states what is to be meditated in these: "bṛhatī." The bṛhatī is a nine-syllable pāda, the bṛhatī metre; and by the "sparśas" — the stop-letters of the ṛcs, those of the five classes from ka onward — and by those ṛcs, Keśava, of manifold forms, is named and to be meditated. (155–156)

अथवा सततं शास्त्रविमर्शेन भविष्यति ।
परमस्नेहसंयुक्तयथार्थज्ञानतो भवेत् ।

#V-END
#T
"Broken (piecemeal) remembrance is dhāraṇā; unbroken (continuous remembrance) is called dhyāna; and, effortlessly, samādhi; and, by that prolonged (samādhi), the (direct) vision. (157) Or else it will come about by constant pondering of the śāstra. That which arises from true knowledge joined with supreme love — that is to be known as bhakti, the means to both enjoyment and liberation. (158)
#T-END

#C
खण्डस्मृतिरिति । एकैकावयवा स्मृतिः खण्डस्मृतिः धारणेत्युच्यते । अखण्डा समग्रावयवस्मृतिः समाधिः ध्यानमित्युच्यते । अप्रयत्ना प्रयत्नं विना समग्रावयवस्थितिः । चिरकालमनुष्ठितया समाधिरूपया स्मृत्या हरेर्दर्शनम् । अपरोक्षज्ञानं भवेदिति भावः । पक्षान्तरमाह । अथवेति । सततं शास्त्रविमर्शेन निरन्तरशास्त्रार्थविचारेण वा दर्शनं अपरोक्षज्ञानं विष्णोर्भविष्यति । भक्तिस्वरूपमाह । परमेति ॥ १५७-१५८ ॥
#C-END
#CT
"Khaṇḍasmṛti": remembrance of one limb at a time is broken remembrance; it is called dhāraṇā. Unbroken — remembrance of all the limbs together — is samādhi, called dhyāna. "Aprayatnā" — the standing (in remembrance) of all the limbs without effort. By remembrance in the form of samādhi, practiced for a long time, comes the vision of Hari — the direct knowledge (aparokṣa-jñāna), such is the sense. He states another alternative: "athavā." Or by constant pondering of the śāstra — by unceasing investigation of the meaning of the śāstra — the vision, the direct knowledge, of Viṣṇu will come about. He states the nature of bhakti: "parama." (157–158)

#C-END
#CT
Teaching his disciples that a book should not be concluded merely by the statement of one's own nature, he performs an auspicious (closing) in the form of a salutation to his own unconditioned, cherished Lord: "aśeṣa." The construction is "with reverence I bow." "Viśeṣāpagatoru-rūpam": "uru-rūpa" means of many forms; and thus, by the viśeṣa (the particular), which produces the effect of difference within non-difference, He possesses many differentiations — such is the meaning. Or the compound may be resolved: He whose forms are, by the viśeṣa (as) gradation, free of (mutual) voidness. And thus the purport is that all His forms are equal — this is the wholly supreme auspiciousness. (165)
