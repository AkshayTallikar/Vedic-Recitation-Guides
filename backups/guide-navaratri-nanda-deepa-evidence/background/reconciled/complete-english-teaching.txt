B03 Ancestors, family deity and gratitude
Look carefully at the beginning of Navarātri. We begin the worship of the family deity after completing the worship of the ancestors. Consider how Nāndi is called at weddings and upanayana ceremonies: after puṇyāha-vācana and worship of the feet, Nāndi is called; after calling Nāndi, the family deity is seated. Navarātri is not merely worship of Durgā, Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī. Whatever our family deity is—Veṅkaṭaramaṇa, Narasiṃha or another deity—each family has its own deity. B04 The three sets of three days
Navarātri is a time—how shall we put it?—when the climate changes. Therefore observe the vrata and “detox” the body, as the speaker describes it. In it, the first three days are for Durgā. Durgā, Durgati-nāśinī, she is called: she destroys our sorrows and difficult conditions. Thus the first three days remove the bad; the next three days are worship of Mahālakṣmī, so that good things, wealth and prosperity may grow. The next three are for Sarasvatī. When wealth increases, is wealth alone enough? Should we not also be wise? You have earned money; you should know how to spend it. There should be understanding in that matter. That requires knowledge; for knowledge, Sarasvatī. Yet it is not only celebration of the three goddesses.
